Quick verdict

For a narrow cleaning caddy under the sink, start with a handled carry-out caddy when you clean more than one room at a time. Choose a low pull-out basket when the cabinet is deep and the caddy will stay in place. The narrowest product is not automatically best: its handle, bottle compartments, and loaded height must still clear the sink bowl and cabinet opening.

Keep the caddy on the side opposite the P-trap or disposal whenever possible. That leaves a service lane for plumbing and gives you a predictable grab point instead of forcing every bottle into the middle of the cabinet.

Measure this before buying

  • Clear door opening: measure between the hinges, frame, and center stile. The caddy needs to pass this opening when loaded, not just fit on the cabinet floor.
  • Usable side width: measure from the side wall to the nearest pipe, disposal, or valve. This is the width available for bottles and fingers.
  • Usable depth: measure from the inside front frame to the rear wall, subtracting hoses and the P-trap bend.
  • Loaded height: measure from the cabinet floor to the lowest sink bowl, drain, or disposal point. Include the caddy handle and the tallest bottle cap.
  • Lift-out path: make sure you can grip the handle, lift the caddy vertically, and turn it through the door opening without striking the plumbing.

Where to place a narrow caddy

Place the caddy in the side lane with the least plumbing. A centered P-trap usually means choosing the left or right bay instead of the middle. A garbage disposal often makes one side unusable, so test the opposite side with the doors fully open.

Keep a small service gap around shutoff valves and do not block the trap with a rigid bin. The caddy should come out without disconnecting anything, and a spill should not be trapped against the cabinet base.

Simple test

Load a temporary tote with the bottles you actually use. Put it in the proposed side lane, grip the handle, and lift it through the open doors. If the tote twists around a pipe or catches the sink bowl, a narrow product with the same loaded height will fail too.

Common fit mistakes

  • Measuring the cabinet interior but forgetting the clear opening between the door hinges.
  • Checking the empty caddy width without accounting for bottle shoulders, spray triggers, or a raised handle.
  • Putting the caddy directly under the P-trap because that is the visual center of the cabinet.
  • Choosing mesh for heavy bottles that will deform the side walls when carried.
  • Blocking the shutoff valves or storing chemicals where a small leak cannot be spotted.

FAQ

What width should a narrow cleaning caddy under the sink be?

Measure the clear side lane beside the plumbing, then choose a caddy narrower than that measurement. Also check the door opening, bottle bulges, and handle because the listed base width is not the whole working clearance.

Is a handled caddy better than a pull-out basket?

A handled caddy is better when you carry supplies between rooms. A pull-out basket is better when the kit stays under one sink and you need access to the back of a deep cabinet.

Can I store spray bottles in a narrow under-sink caddy?

Yes, if the bottle compartments, loaded height, and handle clear the sink bowl and the door opening. Keep bottles upright and leave the plumbing service lane open.

Where should a cleaning caddy sit around under-sink pipes?

Use the clearest side bay, usually opposite a centered trap or disposal. Do not block shutoff valves, the trap, or the path needed to remove the caddy for inspection.
