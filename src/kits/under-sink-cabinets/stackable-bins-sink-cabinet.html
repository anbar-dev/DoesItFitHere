Quick verdict

For stackable bins in a sink cabinet, start with two short bins in one clear side bay rather than filling the full cabinet width. Stacking is useful when the plumbing occupies the middle or back of the cabinet, but a tall stack becomes frustrating if the top bin cannot be reached without removing the lower one.

The real fit test has four parts: the assembled stack must pass through the door opening, sit beside the pipe footprint, clear the lowest point of the sink bowl, and leave the shutoff valves and trap accessible. A bin that fits on the floor but blocks service access is not a working fit.

Measure this before buying

  • Clear door opening: measure the narrowest opening between the cabinet frame, hinges, and center stile. Compare it with the widest assembled bin or stack component.
  • Usable side width: measure from the cabinet wall to the nearest drain, P-trap, disposal, supply line, or valve. This is the width you actually own.
  • Usable depth: measure from the inside front frame to the rear wall, then subtract hoses, rear valves, and any pipe bend that projects forward.
  • Vertical clearance: measure from the floor to the lowest part of the sink bowl, disposal, or drain. Compare this with the planned stack height, not just one bin's height.
  • Reach and access: confirm that you can lift the upper bin out and still turn the shutoff valves. A stack needs hand clearance above it, not only ceiling clearance.

How to stack around sink plumbing

Map the drain, P-trap, disposal, supply lines, and shutoff valves from above before placing anything. The goal is a vertical column in a clear bay, not a stack that straddles the plumbing. If the P-trap is centered, test one stack on the left and then the right; if the disposal hangs on one side, the opposite side is usually the first lane to measure.

Put heavy cleaners and refill bottles in the lower bin. Keep the upper bin for lighter items that you can lift out quickly. Leave a service gap around the trap and valves, and do not let a bin press against a flexible hose or disposal wiring.

Simple test

Use painter's tape to mark one bin footprint on the cabinet floor and another directly above it. Open both doors, reach over the front frame, and pretend to lift the upper bin out. If your hand hits the sink bowl, hinge, pipe, or door, the stack is too tall or too deep for daily use.

Common fit mistakes

  • Measuring the shelf or cabinet box instead of the clear door opening.
  • Adding two bin heights without leaving hand clearance above the upper bin.
  • Using the product's outside dimensions without checking the assembled stack and selected variation.
  • Stacking directly over the P-trap, disposal, supply lines, or shutoff handles.
  • Putting heavy liquid bottles in the top bin, making service access awkward and the stack less stable.

FAQ

Are stackable bins good under a sink?

They can be, when the bins sit in a clear side bay and the upper bin remains reachable. They are a poor choice when the stack blocks the trap, disposal, or shutoff valves.

How tall can stackable bins be under a sink?

Measure from the cabinet floor to the lowest part of the sink bowl or disposal, then keep hand clearance above the upper bin. The usable stack height is lower than the cabinet's interior height.

How do I stack bins around under-sink pipes?

Place the stack beside the pipe footprint rather than over it. Map the plumbing from above, test both side bays, and leave the trap and valve handles serviceable.

Should heavy cleaners go in the top bin?

No. Keep heavy liquid bottles low and use the upper bin for lighter items that can be removed quickly when the plumbing needs inspection.
