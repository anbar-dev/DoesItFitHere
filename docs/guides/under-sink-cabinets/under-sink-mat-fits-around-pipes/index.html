Quick verdict

The safest choice for an under-sink mat that fits around pipes is a waterproof silicone tray with a raised lip that can be trimmed to the cabinet floor. A mat that is slightly smaller is usually easier to fit than one that folds up the sides and buckles around the drain.

Measure the cabinet floor after removing the old liner, then mark the pipe locations on a cardboard template. The mat should lie flat around the drain and shutoff valves without covering access points or pushing the cabinet door outward.

Measure this before buying

  • Usable floor width: measure between the cabinet sides or face-frame obstructions at the front, middle, and back.
  • Usable floor depth: measure from the inside of the door to the back wall. Include the rear corners and any fixed shelf or support.
  • Pipe locations: mark the drain, supply lines, disposal body, and any pipe that rises through the cabinet floor. Measure each obstruction from two edges.
  • Mat thickness: check whether a raised lip or thick edge will interfere with the door, hinges, pull-out shelf, or a low cabinet track.
  • Sink bowl height: confirm that the mat can sit flat under the bowl and that its edge does not press against plumbing when the cabinet door closes.

How to make a clean pipe cutout

  1. Make a cardboard template from the cabinet floor and label the front, back, left, and right before transferring any marks.
  2. Place the template around the plumbing and mark the smallest opening that lets the mat sit flat. Do not cut a wide U-shape when a small round or rectangular notch will work.
  3. Transfer the marks to the mat, keeping cuts away from the raised leak-catching edge whenever possible.
  4. Trim gradually, test-fit the mat, and check the door and plumbing access before making the final cut.

A mat is not a plumbing repair

Use a liner to protect the cabinet from small drips and spills, not to hide an active leak. Keep shutoff valves, drain joints, and cleanouts reachable for inspection.

Common fit mistakes

  • Measuring the cabinet opening instead of the usable floor between the side walls.
  • Cutting around the pipe before making a cardboard template.
  • Removing a large section of the raised edge when a small notch would clear the pipe.
  • Ignoring mat thickness at the front lip, hinge, or pull-out organizer.
  • Covering shutoff valves or drain joints so a small leak cannot be inspected quickly.
  • Using a liner to conceal an active plumbing leak instead of fixing the source.

FAQ

How do I fit an under-sink mat around pipes?

Trace the cabinet floor on cardboard, mark the pipes from two edges, and transfer only the smallest required notch to a trim-to-fit mat. Test the mat flat before making the final cut.

Should an under-sink mat go up the cabinet sides?

A low raised lip can help contain drips, but forcing a large mat up the walls can buckle it and interfere with the door. Match the mat to the floor first.

What size under-sink mat do I need?

Measure the usable floor width and depth, then choose a mat at least large enough to cover that footprint without folding. A larger mat is useful only if it can be trimmed cleanly.

Can an under-sink mat stop a plumbing leak?

No. It can protect the cabinet from small drips and spills, but active leaks should be repaired and the plumbing should remain accessible for inspection.
