Quick verdict

For a trash can under a sink cabinet, the key measurement is not only the bin's width. The can must pass the cabinet opening, clear the hinges, sit below the sink bowl and plumbing, and leave enough room for the door to close and the bag to come out.

A short open-top bin is the safest starting point when the cabinet has a low sink bowl or a tight door. A step can is convenient only if the pedal and lid can operate without the door acting as a stop. If the cabinet is deep but awkward to reach, a low pull-out basket can be easier than a tall lidded can.

Measure this before buying

  • Clear cabinet opening: measure between the door edges or face-frame stiles with the doors open. Use the narrowest width, not the nominal cabinet size.
  • Hinge and door thickness: note how far the hinges project into the cabinet and how much of the opening the door occupies while it swings. A bin that fits through the opening may not fit behind the open hinge.
  • Usable floor area: measure around the P-trap, shutoff valves, disposal, water lines, shelf supports, and cabinet base. Keep the can away from anything that could snag the bag or block service access.
  • Height to the lowest obstruction: measure from the cabinet floor to the sink bowl, drain, disposal, or brace. Leave room for the lid, liner rim, and the hand clearance needed to lift a full bag.
  • Door and lid movement: open the cabinet door fully and trace the lid, pedal, sensor zone, or handle. A swing lid needs air above it; a step pedal needs space in front of the can.
  • Bag-removal path: measure the vertical and diagonal route from the bin to the doorway. You may need to tilt a full liner around the sink bowl or hinge before it can come out.
  • Adjacent door clearance: check the neighboring cabinet or drawer. Two doors can collide when the under-sink doors are open, even if the bin itself stays inside its cabinet.
Test with painter tape: mark the can's outside footprint on the cabinet floor, then open both cabinet doors and mimic pressing the pedal, lifting the lid, and removing a bag. If your hand cannot complete those motions, the bin does not fit in use.

Common fit mistakes

  • Measuring the cabinet box instead of the clear opening between the hinges or face frame.
  • Checking the bin's closed height but forgetting lid, pedal, sensor, or hand clearance.
  • Placing the can directly under the P-trap, disposal, or sink drain because the floor looks empty.
  • Ignoring the diagonal route needed to lift a full liner out of the cabinet.
  • Assuming a cabinet door can stay partly open while the bin is used.
  • Putting a pull-out basket where the shutoff valves or plumbing need service access.
  • Choosing a round bin for a rectangular cabinet and losing useful corner space.

FAQ

How do I measure an under-sink cabinet for a trash can?

Measure the clear opening with the doors open, the usable floor around the plumbing, the height to the sink or lowest obstruction, and the space needed to operate the lid or pedal and remove a full bag.

Can a trash can fit under a sink if the doors have hinges inside?

Sometimes. Measure around the hinge projection and confirm that the can can pass through the opening, sit behind the hinge, and still leave enough room for the doors to close.

Should an under-sink trash can have a lid?

Only if the cabinet has overhead clearance for the lid and enough front space to open it. A low open-top bin may fit better when the sink bowl or door limits the swing.

What is the best trash can for a cabinet with pipes?

A short or narrow bin that sits beside the plumbing is usually safer than a wide container under the P-trap. Measure the pipe zone first and keep service valves reachable.

Is a pull-out trash can better than a freestanding bin under the sink?

A pull-out can improve access, but only when the cabinet floor, slide path, hinges, and door opening all support it. A freestanding bin is easier to remove and reposition if the fit is uncertain.
