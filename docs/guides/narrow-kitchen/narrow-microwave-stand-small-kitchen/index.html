Quick verdict

For a narrow microwave stand for a small kitchen, start with the microwave's outside width and depth, then add a little working margin. A stand that is 13 to 16 inches deep can be useful in a tight kitchen, but only if the microwave does not overhang the front and the rear vents, plug, and cord still have room.

Open baker's racks are usually the easiest fit because you can route the cord and adjust shelf height. A rolling cart adds flexibility, but wheels and a handle increase the footprint. If the stand will sit beside a refrigerator or counter, measure the walkway with the microwave door open, not just the empty gap.

Measure this before buying

  • Microwave footprint: measure the widest point, including feet, handles, and any rear bulge. Use the actual appliance, not the box label.
  • Usable shelf width: check the interior space between side rails, not the rack's overall width. Leave room to set the microwave down without catching a frame.
  • Usable shelf depth: compare the microwave depth with the board or wire shelf depth. Do not count a rear crossbar or a front lip as usable support.
  • Ventilation: follow the microwave manufacturer's side, top, and rear clearance requirements. An open rack does not remove the need for airflow.
  • Outlet and cord path: find the outlet before placing the rack. Allow the plug to bend without forcing the microwave against the wall or a shelf brace.
  • Door and control access: tape out the microwave door swing and leave space to reach the handle and control panel without standing in the main kitchen lane.
  • Walkway and appliance depth: measure the remaining passage beside the rack, especially next to a refrigerator door, dishwasher, island, or cabinet pull.
  • Floor and leveling: check baseboards, thresholds, and uneven floors. Leveling feet can help a fixed rack; casters need locks and a flat path.
Write the fit envelope first: for example, “maximum 24 inches wide, 16 inches deep, 40 inches high, with 3 inches of rear clearance and a clear door swing.” Shop to those usable limits.

Common fit mistakes

  • Measuring the rack's outside width but not the clear shelf between its rails.
  • Assuming a 13-inch stand depth is enough without measuring the microwave's rear bulge and plug.
  • Placing the microwave under a cabinet or shelf without checking the manufacturer's top clearance.
  • Letting a door, control panel, or handle open into the only kitchen walkway.
  • Using a cart with unlocked wheels for an appliance that is loaded and unloaded frequently.
  • Routing the power cord across a hot cooktop, sink, or traffic path.
  • Choosing a tall rack without planning wall anchoring or checking the ceiling and cabinet height.

FAQ

How deep should a microwave stand be?

Start with the microwave's actual depth and add room for the plug, rear ventilation, and a stable supported surface. A stand listed at 13 to 16 inches deep may work for a compact microwave, but the appliance footprint decides.

Can a baker's rack hold a microwave?

Some can, if the shelf is level, strong enough, and wide and deep enough to support the appliance fully. Check the rack's load limit and the microwave manufacturer's clearance instructions before using it.

Is a rolling cart safe for a microwave?

It can be practical on a level floor when the wheels lock and the microwave is centered on a fully supported top. Avoid carts that are narrower than the appliance or that roll into a work lane.

How much space should be left around a microwave?

Use the clearances in your microwave manual rather than a universal number. Measure side, top, and rear space, then include the cord bend and the door swing in the layout.
