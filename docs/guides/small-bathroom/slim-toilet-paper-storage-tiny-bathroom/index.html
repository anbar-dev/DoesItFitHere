Quick verdict

For slim toilet paper storage in a tiny bathroom, start with the empty floor or wall rectangle beside the toilet. Then add the space needed to pull a roll out, lift a lid, open the cabinet door, or reach behind the toilet for cleaning.

A narrow cabinet around 6 to 8 inches wide can use a dead corner without crowding the bowl, while a shallow basket can sit beside the vanity or under a shelf. Over-tank and wall-mounted formats save floor space, but they must clear the tank lid, flush control, seat, and any nearby shelf or mirror.

Measure this before buying

  • Floor gap width: measure the narrowest point beside the toilet, vanity, wall, or shower trim. Check at floor level and at the widest point of the bowl.
  • Usable depth: measure from the wall or vanity front to the furthest edge you can occupy. Include baseboard, handles, feet, and the front of the basket or cabinet.
  • Toilet-lid and seat movement: open the lid and seat fully. A cabinet that fits beside the bowl can still stop the lid or brush a person's shoulder.
  • Roll diameter and access: leave room for the largest roll you plan to store and for a hand to remove it without scraping the wall or fixture.
  • Door and drawer path: check the bathroom entrance, vanity drawers, shower door, and the storage door or lid together. Mark each opening arc on the floor with tape.
  • Tank and flush clearance: for over-tank storage, measure the tank lid, flush button or lever, supply line, and the distance to the wall.
  • Cleaning and moisture: preserve a small cleaning gap where possible and keep paper away from splash zones, wet towels, and the shower spray.
Measure the widest roll: a holder can fit the room but fail at the opening if a mega roll, lid, basket rim, or cabinet door needs more space than the body suggests.

Common fit mistakes

  • Measuring the empty wall gap but forgetting the toilet bowl's widest point.
  • Using a holder's body width without allowing for the roll opening, lid, feet, or handle.
  • Putting a basket where the bathroom door, vanity drawer, or shower door needs to open.
  • Choosing an over-tank cabinet without measuring the tank lid, flush lever, or supply line.
  • Storing paper beside a shower or sink splash zone where rolls can absorb moisture.
  • Buying a deep low basket because its width is narrow, then losing the walking path in front of it.
  • Leaving a tall narrow cabinet unsecured beside the toilet.

FAQ

How narrow can toilet paper storage be?

Measure the actual gap beside the toilet, then compare the widest part of the holder or cabinet. A six-inch-wide cabinet may fit a very tight strip, but the door, roll opening, baseboard, and hand clearance still need room.

Is it better to store toilet paper beside or above the toilet?

Use the location with the clearest access and least moisture. Beside the toilet is easier to load when the floor path is open; above the toilet saves floor space only when the tank lid, flush control, seat, and wall can all remain usable.

How do I keep toilet paper storage dry in a tiny bathroom?

Keep it outside the direct splash path, leave a small cleaning gap, and use a lidded or enclosed format when the room has frequent condensation. Do not press a basket or cabinet against a wet wall.

Can a narrow toilet paper cabinet sit next to the toilet?

Yes, if its body, door, and handle clear the bowl, toilet lid, vanity, and entrance path. Mark the door swing on the floor and check that a person can still sit, stand, and clean around the fixture.
