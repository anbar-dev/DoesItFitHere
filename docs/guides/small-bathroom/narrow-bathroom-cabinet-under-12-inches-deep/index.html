Narrow bathroom cabinet under 12 inches deep.

Quick verdict

For a narrow bathroom cabinet under 12 inches deep, treat 12 inches as a hard outer limit, not the target. A cabinet listed at 11.8 inches deep may still project farther once you add a door, handle, baseboard gap, uneven wall, or the clearance needed to open it.

In most tight rooms, start with a cabinet around 10 to 11 inches deep and compare its full width and height against the wall, toilet, vanity, shower trim, and entrance path. A taller cabinet saves floor space, but only if it can be anchored and its doors or drawers can open without blocking the room.

Measure this before buying

  • Wall-to-front depth: measure from the finished wall to the furthest point the cabinet can occupy. Include baseboard, trim, handles, doors, and feet.
  • Width at the tightest point: measure beside the vanity, toilet, shower casing, or wall corner. Check the top and bottom because a wall or pipe may taper the opening.
  • Door and drawer projection: open the nearby vanity door, bathroom door, shower door, and the cabinet itself. The listed cabinet depth is not the same as the open-door envelope.
  • Height and reach: check ceiling, mirror, sconce, outlet, towel bar, and the height of anything stored on the upper shelf.
  • Baseboard and floor: decide whether the cabinet will sit in front of the baseboard, be notched around it, or need spacers. Check for an uneven floor before using a tall narrow tower.
  • Plumbing and access: keep shutoff valves, cleanouts, electrical outlets, and service panels reachable. Do not cover a connection that needs regular inspection.
  • Anchoring surface: locate studs or use hardware rated for the wall type. A tall shallow cabinet can tip more easily than a deep low unit if it is left unsecured.
Use an installed-depth rule: wall depth + baseboard/trim + the cabinet's front hardware must stay within the measured zone. If the result is close to 12 inches, choose a shallower model or a wall-mounted alternative.

Common fit mistakes

  • Using the cabinet-box depth and forgetting the front door, handle, feet, or baseboard.
  • Checking the cabinet width only at eye level and missing a pipe, trim, or uneven wall near the floor.
  • Choosing a 11.8-inch-deep cabinet for a 12-inch gap with no allowance for trim or installation tolerance.
  • Measuring a hinged door but not marking the full open-door arc on the floor.
  • Forgetting that drawers need a straight pull-out path and cannot share space with a toilet or hamper.
  • Putting a tall cabinet beside a shower without protecting the material from repeated moisture.
  • Leaving a tall, shallow cabinet unsecured because it appears stable when empty.

FAQ

Is a cabinet listed at 12 inches deep safe for a 12-inch gap?

Usually not without more checking. The listing may describe the box only, while the door, handle, baseboard, wall variation, and installation tolerance need additional room. Target a shallower cabinet when the gap is a hard limit.

How deep should a narrow bathroom cabinet be?

Measure the usable wall-to-front zone first. In many tight bathrooms, a cabinet around 10 to 11 inches deep gives more margin than one listed at 11.8 inches, but the correct depth depends on the fixture and opening path beside it.

Are tall narrow bathroom cabinets stable?

They can be stable when assembled on a level floor and anchored to a suitable wall. A shallow, tall cabinet should not be treated like a freestanding object if people, pets, or stored items could pull it forward.

Can a narrow bathroom cabinet sit over a baseboard?

Sometimes, but the baseboard can push the cabinet forward and reduce the real walkway clearance. Measure with the baseboard in place, or plan a proper spacer or notch that keeps the cabinet level and secure.
